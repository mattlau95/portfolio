Overview

Aduro Products makes tech accessories, starting out supplying brick-and-mortar stores before moving into e-commerce — Amazon, Walmart, Groupon, and Tophatter. Amazon drives a large share of the company's sales, and when I was hired in fall of 2018 as a graphic designer, I was assigned to the team dedicated to Amazon listings.

Beyond the flagship Aduro Products (tech accessories) line, the company runs several sister brands — Aduro Sport (home fitness), Olivia Rose (beauty), Hearth & Haven (lighting), and more — and I produced listing graphics for those as well.

Curating Amazon images

"Curator" is a good word for the role. I was responsible for every graphic posted on our new Amazon product listings — making sure images were the correct dimensions and presented in a way that drove conversions, without misleading the customer in a way that would come back as a bad review. Below is a blog-style walkthrough of a few things that role actually involved.

Creating the right group of images

The order a potential customer sees images in matters a lot. Generally the most attractive images go first and the more informational ones go last, though the exact strategy shifts by product.

Same product, different colors

Many Aduro products ship in multiple colors, and there's a method to producing matching image sets across colorways so that nothing changes except the product itself. That takes precision, careful color matching, and — most of all — meticulously organized Photoshop layers.

Photo manipulation

This is the bread and butter of e-commerce graphic design, and the skill that got me hired at Aduro in the first place. Aduro and its sub-brands are always releasing new products, so rather than hiring a model and shooting real "lifestyle" photography for every one, we take product photos or renders and composite them onto stock imagery in Photoshop. Making that look realistic and aesthetic is the whole job — if you've never thought about what goes on behind a product photo on Amazon, we're probably doing it right. The template system

None of this got rebuilt from scratch each time. Each product category runs on a master composite file — background, lighting, shadow, and callout layers stay fixed, while the product art and color variant swap in. That structure is what made it possible to style 100+ products across Aduro Products, Aduro Sport, Olivia Rose, and Hearth & Haven without every listing turning into its own from-scratch shoot: a new SKU is an afternoon inside an existing template, not a new design.

Amazon videos

I also produced video for the listings — some promotional, giving shoppers a closer look at the product; some instructional, walking through setup; and some straightforward unboxings.
